# Desire Lines

*An algorithmic philosophy for worn ground.*

No one draws a desire line. It forms when many travellers each cut the same corner, and the grass gives up where they walk. Desire Lines treats a road as what is left behind by movement, never as a shape someone placed. The algorithm doesn't lay paths down. It sends walkers out and lets the ground remember them. Each piece should look like a meticulously crafted system, tuned over many careful iterations until the trodden earth looks inevitable rather than random.

The walkers are simple. Each one leaves from a gate on the edge of the meadow, sometimes stops at a clearing, and heads for another gate. As it walks it senses the ground ahead, to the left and to the right, and turns towards wherever the grass is already more trodden, like a slime mould following its own chemistry. A steady pull towards its goal stops it orbiting forever. A low-frequency noise makes it wander, so no two journeys match. Early trips scatter. Later ones start to share routes, the shared routes deepen, and a few confident roads emerge from many faint tries. Getting that balance right is the most important tuning decision in the whole piece: the turn rates, sensor reach and trample curve have to be set with the care of a master implementation.

The meadow underneath is a height field of layered noise, lit from the upper left. Every slope facing that light gets brighter and every slope facing away gets darker. The light is then quantised into a few flat value steps, so the ground reads like cut paper and not like a photograph. Light comes from one direction everywhere, the same rule the hand-drawn characters follow, so the ground and the characters on it share one world.

Wear is turned into paint by a careful set of thresholds. Past one level the grass yellows and thins. Past the next it gives way to bare earth, with an ink line where the earth begins, as wide as the character outlines. The two banks are treated differently: the upper-left bank casts a thin shadow into the path and the lower-right bank catches light. The most-travelled middle of a road is the palest strip. Small pebbles settle only in the bare earth. Grass tufts gather densest where the meadow meets the path and spill over its edge. Flowers and mossy stones keep their distance, and each sits on its own small shadow below and to the right.

Each seed is a different history of footsteps across one meadow, from one palette: ink, leaf, cream and gold. The parameters are the questions a landscape designer would ask. How many travellers came? How wide were they? How much did they follow each other, and how much did they wander? How rough is the land? Changing any one of them retells the history. The result should feel like the product of deep computational expertise, a quiet and painstakingly balanced machine for making ground that looks walked on.
