The two winners

Where Is My Mama? (3D song)

3,882 views. It won on reach: YouTube showed its thumbnail 120,313 times, and 93% of its views came from Suggested videos. No other video on the channel has more than 34,632 impressions.

  • Hook: the title is a child's question, and the first sung line repeats it.
  • Story: baby animals look for their mamas with Milo; each verse ends with a find ("There she is!"), the song ends on "Mama is found!".
  • Emotion: a baby, a mama, a happy reunion. Nobody is lost or sad on screen.
  • Pacing: the picture changes every 1.1 seconds on average; the longest it holds still is 7 seconds.
  • Thumbnail: Milo's face fills the middle, three friends around him, the question in big yellow letters.

Its watch time is ordinary: 0:28 average, 16.6% watched, the same as the other 3D songs (12–22%). Click rate 2.9%. 46% of its views came from India. Why YouTube suggested this one so widely is not something Studio shows; the title and topic are what set it apart from the other songs.

Camel Humps Are Full of Fat, Not Water! (cartoon Short)

4,913 views, the most on the channel. 94% came from the Shorts feed. 64.6% of the people who saw it stayed to watch, and it was watched 72.6% through.

  • Hook: the animal is on screen in the first frame and Milo asks "What do you think is inside a camel's hump? Is it water?" in the first 3 seconds.
  • Payoff: the answer lands at 0:09 with a label on the hump ("FAT, NOT WATER!").
  • Story: one animal, one myth turned over, two small extra facts, then "Can you blink your eyes like a camel?"
  • Pacing: one location, but something new appears every 3.5 seconds (a question mark, Milo, the label, a zoom).
  • Title: the surprising fact itself, 39 characters.

Published Nov 7. Almost all of its views came after Nov 9 (3,206 by Nov 13, 4,538 by Nov 14). 33% of its views came from the Philippines, 14% India, 13% United States.

Going back to cartoon does not fix the stillness by itself. The old cartoon explainers sit in long still stretches for about half of their length, the same as the 3D explainers. The fix is the shot plan: the winning song changes its picture three times as often as the 3D explainers and five times as often as the old cartoon ones.

The five rules for every new cartoon video

  1. Start with a question a child wants answered, with the animal already on screen.Explainers ask it in the first 5 seconds; Shorts answer it by 0:10. Ask the viewer something again about every 45 seconds, and end with a recap. (Camel: question at 0:00, answer at 0:09.)
  2. One true, surprising fact carries the video, and the title says it.Eight of the top ten videos are one surprising fact ("Camel Humps Are Full of Fat, Not Water!", "An Octopus Has 3 Hearts!"). Every fact is read at its source the day the script is written.
  3. Tell it as a small story with a baby and its mama.A want, a search with Milo, a happy find. Nobody lost, hurt, scared or crying. (Where Is My Mama? is the only long video YouTube suggested widely.)
  4. Change the picture every 4 to 6 seconds, never longer than 8.A new place, a camera move, a close-up, a cutaway, a diagram, a comparison or an on-screen word. At least 6 places per explainer; a change every 2 to 3 seconds in a Short. The pace check rejects any stretch over 8 seconds.
  5. Show the fact, not the speaker.Every fact line gets its own picture of exactly that thing, in sync with the words. (At 4:03 of Animal Champions the spots are spoken and never shown.)
