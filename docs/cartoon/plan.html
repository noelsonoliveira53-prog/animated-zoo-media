What one cartoon video costs to make, measured today. The 5:04 lesson rendered in 37 minutes and the 1:28 Short in 13 minutes on the machine this session runs on. Four long cartoon videos and three Shorts a day would be about 3 hours of rendering. Writing an episode, reading its facts and looking at a still of every shot took longer than the rendering, and whether that keeps pace with four a day has not been tried.

Why these formats

The two winners point the same way. "Where Is My Mama?" (3,882 views, the best 3D video) is a song with a baby, a mama and a feeling. The camel Short (4,913 views, the best video on the channel) is one animal and one surprising fact, with a new picture every 3.5 seconds. The 3D explainers stood still for a third to a half of their length. So every format below carries a story with a baby or a friend at its centre, one true fact that surprises, and a picture that keeps changing. The numbers and frames are on the findings page.

Format: stories and adventures

Series name: Milo's Zoo Adventures. Minimum 5:00, at most 6:30, native 4K.

  • Hook: the problem and the feeling, in the first 5 seconds
  • Milo's opening line
  • The goal
  • Song: off we go
  • Obstacle 1
  • Obstacle 2
  • Obstacle 3
  • The low moment
  • The friends find the answer
  • Song: home we go
  • Warm ending
  • The ending song line

What comes back every episode

  • Milo's opening line: "I'm Milo! Come on a big adventure with us!"
  • The refrain, sung on the way out and on the way home: "Friends help friends, come along with me!"
  • The ending song line: "Friends help friends, hooray, hooray! What a big adventure day!"
  • Milo's goodbye: "See you on the next adventure, friends!"
  • A new place and a new problem each time.

What every story must have

  • At least 6 places or set-ups, and something new on screen every 4 to 6 seconds.
  • 2 or 3 true animal facts inside the plot. Each one solves a problem, and each one gets a picture board.
  • Acting: the characters' faces change (happy, worried, surprised), they look at each other, they walk through the scene.
  • One or two questions to the child, with time to answer.
  • The answer comes from the characters, never from a grown-up or by luck.

Values and limits

  • Kindness, courage, friendship, curiosity.
  • No villains. The problem is a river, a tall tree, a shy feeling, a present that went wrong.
  • Nobody is hurt, lost, crying or trapped. The low moment is a worried face and friends coming close.
  • Every problem is solved before the ending. Nothing a child could copy and get hurt.

Format: animal facts

Minimum 5:00, at most 6:30, native 4K. Three parts of about a minute and a half, each cut into one Short.

Shape

  • Opens with the question and the animal on screen, inside the first 5 seconds.
  • A mission, not a lecture: Milo and a baby animal go and find out.
  • A question to the child about every 45 seconds, with two answer signs and thinking time.
  • A quiz and a recap of three things at the end.

Picture

  • A new shot or change every 4 to 6 seconds: a new place, a camera move, a close-up, a cutaway, a diagram, a comparison, a word.
  • Every fact has its own picture: the hump cut open, the clock, the scale, the eye with two rows of lashes.
  • Diagrams sit on a picture board, never floating in a scene.
  • Words on screen appear on the spoken word.

Facts

  • Each fact is read at its source on the day the script is written. The sentence is kept in the episode file.
  • If the source cannot be read, the line comes out.
  • The sources are named in the description.

Format: music (3D, unchanged)

Keep

  • The current 3D look and pace. "Where Is My Mama?" changes its picture about every second.
  • The 3:30 PM slot.
  • No Shorts cut from songs.

Write more like the winner

  • One character from the cast, one feeling, a small journey with a beginning and an arrival.
  • A chorus of one line a child can repeat after hearing it once.
  • The song of the week belongs to that week's friend, so the song, the adventures and the lessons point at each other.

Format: Shorts

Shape

  • About 90 seconds, vertical, 4K.
  • One animal. One surprising fact or one story moment.
  • The title asks the question; the answer is the last thing in the video.

Picture

  • The first frame is the strangest picture in the video, with the question on it.
  • A change every 2 to 3 seconds.
  • Each shot is framed again for the tall picture, with its own camera. Words and props move to fit. It is not a crop.

Where they come from

  • From adventures and fact lessons only.
  • A fact Short is one part of a lesson, cut whole.
  • A story Short is the low moment and the answer of an adventure.

The first block of content

Five themes. Each theme belongs to one friend and one place, and holds 3 adventures, 3 fact lessons, 2 story songs and 4 Shorts.

Facts marked read were read at their source today. Facts marked to read are ideas: they are read at the source on the day the script is written, and the line comes out if the source does not say it. Farm (Clover the lamb) and Night forest (Hazel the hedgehog) are the next two themes; they are not written yet.
